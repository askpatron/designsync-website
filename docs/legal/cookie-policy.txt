This explains the cookies we set and the ones other companies set through us. It
covers our marketing site at trydesignsync.com and our client portal at
app.trydesignsync.com. It sits alongside our Privacy Policy, which explains

The short version. Our marketing site sets no cookies of its own. Our portal sets
three, and it needs all three to sign you in and keep your account safe. We run no
advertising cookies, no analytics cookies, and nothing that follows you around the
internet. That is why you are not reading this over the top of a consent banner.


1. WHAT A COOKIE IS

A small file a website asks your browser to keep, so it can recognise your browser
the next time it sees it. Some are necessary: without one, a site cannot tell that
the person loading this page is the same person who logged in a minute ago. Others
exist to build a picture of what you do across many different sites, which is what
advertising cookies are for.

We use the first kind. Today we use none of the second kind.


2. OUR MARKETING SITE (trydesignsync.com)

We set no cookies of our own here. There is nothing to accept and nothing to
refuse.

Two other companies are involved in loading this site, and you should know about
both:

  Google Fonts serves the typeface. It sets no cookie, but your browser has to ask
  Google for the font file, so Google sees your IP address and the fact that you
  loaded a page here. We can remove this entirely by hosting the font ourselves.

  Tawk.to runs the live chat, and only if you click to open it. Its script is not
  on the page until then. Once you open the chat, Tawk.to sets its own cookies so
  it can recognise you between messages and keep your conversation together. Those
  cookies are Tawk.to's, and its policy is at tawk.to/privacy-policy. If you never
  open the chat, none of them are set.

  That is deliberate. The chat is useful, and it is also a third party that would
  otherwise watch every visitor for the sake of the few who ever say hello. Making
  it load on click means your click is the permission.


3. OUR PORTAL (app.trydesignsync.com)

Three cookies, all ours, all necessary. There is no version of a working login
that does not use them.

  designsync-session
  What it does: signs you in and keeps you signed in while you use the portal.
  Lasts: 120 minutes of inactivity.

  XSRF-TOKEN
  What it does: proves a form was submitted from our page and not from a hostile
  site pretending to be you. It is a security control, not a tracker.
  Lasts: your browsing session.

  remember_web_[hash]
  What it does: keeps you signed in between visits. Set only if you tick
  "remember me" when you log in. If you do not tick it, it is never set.
  Lasts: a long time, which is the point of it. Log out to clear it.

None of these follow you to any other website. They are readable only by us, and
they contain no information about you beyond an identifier for your session.


4. WHAT WE DO NOT USE

Today, and stated plainly because it is unusual enough to be worth stating:

  No advertising or retargeting pixels
  No third party analytics, no Google Analytics, no Meta Pixel
  No data brokers, no audience sharing, no data sales
  No cookies that follow you to any other website

If you have ever looked at a product once and then seen it chase you across the
internet for a fortnight, that is the thing we are not doing.


5. IF WE START ADVERTISING

  OWNER: READ THIS BEFORE YOU ADD THE PIXEL, NOT AFTER.

  This section is deliberately empty. If a Meta Pixel goes onto the marketing
  site, it gets filled in and four things happen at once:

  1. Section 4 above becomes false, and so do four separate statements in the
     Privacy Policy. All of them have to be rewritten in the same change, not
     later. Publishing "we run no advertising cookies" while running one is the
     kind of statement that turns a small problem into a real one.

  2. You need a consent banner, and the pixel must not load until someone
     accepts. This is the whole reason we do not have one today. A pixel is not
     necessary for the site to work, it exists to track people for advertising,
     and no honest reading of "legitimate interest" under section 25 of the NDPA
     covers it. Consent is the only basis that fits. It also has to be a real
     choice: refusing must be as easy as accepting, and refusing must actually
     stop the pixel loading.

     You told me you intend to expand to other regions. The day you run an ad
     that a European sees, ePrivacy and the GDPR apply to that visitor, and both
     are much stricter than the NDPA about exactly this. Building the banner
     properly the first time is cheaper than retrofitting it under pressure.

  3. Meta becomes a recipient of your visitors' data, in the United States, and
     the Privacy Policy has to name it in section 3 and account for the transfer
     under Part IX. Meta is not a company anyone regards as a cautious steward of
     personal data, and that is now your disclosure to make, not theirs.

  4. The banner will cost you conversions on the paid traffic the pixel exists to
     measure. That is a real trade, not a technicality. It is worth making with
     your eyes open.

  WHAT I RECOMMEND INSTEAD, OR AT LEAST ALONGSIDE.

  The thing you actually want is to know which ads produce paying clients. A
  browser pixel is a poor way to get that and getting worse: iOS blocks a lot of
  it, ad blockers take more, and the pixel cannot see the payment anyway, because
  the payment happens on Paystack.

  Meta's Conversions API sends the conversion from our server instead of from the
  visitor's browser. We already have the exact moment a purchase completes: the
  Paystack webhook. Sending it from there is more accurate than a pixel, survives
  ad blockers, and never puts a third party script on your checkout.

  It is not a consent-free option. It is still personal data going to Meta, so it
  still needs a lawful basis and a disclosure. But it is the better instrument for
  the job, and it keeps the tracking off the pages where a stranger is typing
  their phone number into a form.

  My suggestion: pixel on the marketing site behind a genuine consent banner, the
  Conversions API from the Paystack webhook for the numbers you will actually
  make decisions on, and nothing whatsoever on the portal, where people are
  logged in and working.

  Decide, and I will write this section and fix the Privacy Policy in one change.


6. CONTROLLING COOKIES

Every browser lets you see the cookies a site has set, delete them, and refuse
new ones. Look in the privacy or security section of your browser's settings.

Be aware of the trade: the three cookies in section 3 are how the portal knows who
you are. Block them and you cannot log in. That is not us being difficult, it is
what those cookies are for.

To get rid of the chat's cookies, clear the site's cookies in your browser.


7. DO NOT TRACK AND GLOBAL PRIVACY CONTROL

Some browsers can send a signal asking sites not to track you. There is no agreed
standard for what a site must do about it, and most sites ignore it.

We do not need to honour it, because we do not track you in the first place. If
that ever changes, we will honour a Global Privacy Control signal as a refusal of
consent, and this section will say so specifically rather than in the abstract.


8. CHANGES

cookie that is not strictly necessary, we will not do it quietly: you will be
